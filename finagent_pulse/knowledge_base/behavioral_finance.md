# Behavioural Finance — Traps and Biases

## Bull Trap
A sharp upward move during a structural downtrend that draws in buyers before
resuming the decline. Diagnostic signature: price rallies on strong positive
headline sentiment while the trend remains below its long-term moving average
and volume fails to confirm.

## Bear Trap
A sharp decline that triggers stop-losses and panic selling before the market
recovers. Diagnostic signature: extremely negative sentiment, elevated
volatility, but a forecast and underlying trend that remain intact. This is the
classic case where the sentiment reading and the quantitative model diverge.

## Narrative-Driven Overreaction
Markets systematically overreact to vivid, easily narrated events and underreact
to slow-moving numerical information. A single dramatic headline should never be
treated with the same weight as a persistent multi-day shift in aggregate
sentiment.

## Recency and Availability Bias
Recent and memorable events are over-weighted in forecasts. This is precisely
why a sentiment index must be normalised against its own historical
distribution rather than read on an absolute scale.

## Herding
When news coverage volume spikes far above its baseline, prices reflect
attention as much as fundamentals. A headline-count z-score above 2 signals a
crowded, attention-driven regime in which mean reversion becomes more likely.

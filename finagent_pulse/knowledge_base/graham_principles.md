# Benjamin Graham — Defensive Investing Principles

## Margin of Safety
The central concept of investment. Never buy on the assumption that a forecast
will be correct; buy only when the price is far enough below conservatively
estimated value that being wrong still leaves capital intact. A forecast with a
narrow expected edge does not justify a full-size position.

## Mr. Market
The market is a manic-depressive business partner who quotes a price every day.
His quotes are there to serve you, not to instruct you. Extreme quotations are
opportunities, not information about intrinsic value. A sharp drop on sentiment
alone, with no change in underlying earnings power, is a Mr. Market episode.

## Speculation versus Investment
An investment operation promises safety of principal and an adequate return.
Anything else is speculation. Acting on a 7-day price forecast is speculation
unless the position is sized so that an adverse outcome is survivable.

## Defensive Allocation Rule
The defensive investor holds between 25% and 75% in equities, adjusting toward
the lower bound when the market is euphoric and prices are historically high,
and toward the upper bound when the market is depressed. Never 0% and never
100%: neither total conviction nor total fear is justified.

## Consistency and Discipline
The investor's chief problem, and even their worst enemy, is likely to be
themselves. Rules decided in calm conditions must not be revised in panic.

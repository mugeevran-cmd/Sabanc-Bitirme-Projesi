# Institutional Risk Management Guidelines

## Position Sizing Under Uncertainty
Position size should scale with the confidence-to-volatility ratio, not with
the magnitude of the forecast alone. When realised volatility sits in the top
quintile of its trailing distribution, cut standard position size by at least
half regardless of signal strength.

## Signal Confirmation Requirement
A directional trade requires agreement between at least two independent
evidence streams — for example a quantitative price forecast and a news
sentiment reading. When the quantitative model and the sentiment engine
disagree, the correct action is HOLD, not an averaged position. Disagreement is
itself information: it indicates an unstable regime.

## Drawdown Discipline
Predefine the invalidation level before entering. If the 7-day forecast implies
a move smaller than the instrument's typical 7-day volatility, the trade has no
edge net of transaction costs and should be skipped.

## Regime Awareness
Volatility clusters. A high-volatility session is far more likely to be followed
by another high-volatility session than by a calm one. Forecast intervals must
be widened, and conviction reduced, when entering a high-volatility regime.

## Crowding and Sentiment Extremes
Uniformly one-sided news sentiment is a contrarian warning, not a confirmation.
When the share of negative headlines exceeds roughly 70%, the bearish case is
typically already reflected in the price, and the asymmetry of outcomes begins
to favour the upside.

### Risk Manager — Final Directive

## `HOLD`  ·  position size **0.0%** of standard  ·  conviction **50%**

The committee holds, and it holds on magnitude alone. The Bi-LSTM projects **+0.26%** over the next seven sessions against an expected move of **1.90%** for that horizon — a signal-to-noise ratio of **0.14** where the conviction floor calibrated on the validation period is **0.18**. The forecast points up, and the sentiment channel does not contradict it; the two streams are **sentiment neutral**, not in conflict. What fails is the size of the edge. The model is not saying the index falls. It is saying it cannot separate +0.26% from the noise of an ordinary week, and a position taken on that separation is a position taken on rounding.

This was not a wide miss, and the distinction is worth stating. A forecast **1.29x larger — about +0.33% instead of +0.26%** — would have cleared the floor, and with the streams neutral rather than opposed, nothing else stood in the way: had the gate passed, the committee would have issued a BUY. A HOLD that came within seven basis points of a trade carries different information from one that was never close. This should be read as a marginal opportunity declined, not as a risk identified.

The retrieved coverage supports the same conclusion from the other side. Hybrid retrieval over the trailing fourteen sessions returned eight headlines, and they do not describe a market in trouble — they describe a market being asked whether it has come too far, by people who disagree. *"Daily record highs across the S&P 500 are telling investors this rally is broadening"* (2024-02-29, +0.83) sits in the same window as JP Morgan's *"Is S&P 500 in a bubble zone? ... the index's rally is at risk"* (2024-03-01, −0.37), and as Bank of America's observation that the index's valuation is in the 95th percentile versus history while investors *"shouldn't worry"* (2024-02-28, −0.21). *"The S&P 500 is overbought, but that doesn't mean it's time to sell"* (2024-02-29) is the argument compressed into a single headline. Both cases are already articulated in public and neither has won. That is not a tape to take a marginal position into.

Two readings should be held together rather than reconciled. Fear & Greed stands at **88.3/100** — the greed band — while the 31 headlines attached to today score **−0.161**, with 41.9% of coverage negative against 25.8% positive. The index is a twenty-day smoothed percentile and today is not, so this is a turn beginning rather than a contradiction: positioning is still complacent while the news flow has already rolled over. Meanwhile an index at **5,130.95** trades **4.8%** above its 50-day average with RSI at **69**, and coverage runs **1.4 sigma** above its sixty-day norm. Nothing here is disorderly. Everything here says the market has run and is being watched for it.

Three of the principles retrieved for this state converge on the same instruction. **Margin of Safety** holds that one must never buy on the assumption that a forecast will be correct, and that a forecast with a narrow expected edge does not justify a full-size position — the edge here is 0.26% against a 1.90% band. **Position Sizing Under Uncertainty** holds that size scales with the confidence-to-volatility ratio rather than with the direction of the call; at a ratio of 0.14 that scaling returns zero. **Crowding and Sentiment Extremes** is the one to watch rather than act on: it treats uniformly one-sided coverage as a contrarian warning, and at 41.9% negative this coverage is not one-sided. The crowding is visible in the Fear & Greed reading, not yet in the headlines — which is the sequence in which these things usually arrive.

**Invalidation frame.** The call is scoped to **seven trading sessions** against a **1.90%** noise band. Inside that band, nothing that happens is evidence about this decision. Outside it, the thesis is void. One asymmetry deserves flagging for the next session: with greed at 88 sitting above a news flow that has already turned, a surprise is more likely to arrive from the downside than the +0.26% projection implies. That is a reason to keep the abstention, not a reason to reverse it.
